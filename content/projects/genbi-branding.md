## Brief

Present GenBI as a community with a clear vision and a directed target, with a design that is easy to remember — a first step toward the 2025 target, starting from internal management and GenBI Gorontalo's social-media presence.

## The challenge

GenBI Gorontalo brings together Bank Indonesia scholarship recipients from three campus commissariats, organised into seven departments. With that many people posting, the risk was every post looking like it came from a different organisation. As chairman I could set the direction; as the designer I had to make it something people would actually reuse.

## The vision, as a mark

The 2025 vision and values were formulated as:

> Becoming the best new generation of Indonesia in Sulawesi, holding the values of kinship, mutual protection, determination, professionalism, and activeness (KESATRIA).

That vision became a logo — **Patuju Kesatria 2025** — repeated across GenBI Gorontalo posts as a reminder. An upright, pointed form is lifted by a pair of wings resting on curling waves: one silhouette that still reads at the size of a footer icon.

## Seven symbols, one family

Each department received its own symbol. They share one construction — a circle (often broken open), a single stroke weight with rounded ends, and one navy — so any one of them is recognisably GenBI, and together they read as a set.

## Palette

A deep navy carries the symbols and headings. Red, green and yellow appear only as a thin stripe at the bottom of each template — enough color to feel lively, never enough to compete with the content.

## The system in use

**Feed template.** Every post shares the same frame: partner logos in the header (Bank Indonesia, GenBI Gorontalo and the three campuses), a light grid for the content, and a footer with the vision mark, social handles and the color stripe. Whoever makes the post only fills the middle.

## Looking back

The strongest decision was keeping every symbol on the same construction — it is why seven different ideas still look like one organisation. If I did it again, I would write the rules down as a one-page guideline (clear space, minimum sizes, when to use the stripe) so the next board could keep the system going without me.
